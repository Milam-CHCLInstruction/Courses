Course Overview

This presentation covers the minimum requirements for obtaining an Arkansas Concealed Handgun Carry License (CHCL). Minimum instruction time is 5 hours excluding range live fire, fingerprinting, and application completion.

Important Note: Arkansas Code Â§ 5-73-329 states that this subchapter does NOT require a person to obtain a license to carry a concealed handgun in Arkansas. The license provides reciprocity in other states.

Course Objectives

  • Understand Arkansas and Federal firearms laws
  • Learn proper handgun safety and handling
  • Know when deadly force is legally justified
  • Demonstrate proficiency with live fire
  • Understand responsibilities of license holders

Part I: Administrative Matters

License Reciprocity

The Arkansas CHCL is recognized in many other states. However, you are responsible for:

  • Determining which states honor your Arkansas license
  • Understanding each state's specific requirements and restrictions
  • Complying with all laws of states you visit

Pass/Fail Criteria

To successfully complete this course, you must:

  • Attend all classroom instruction (minimum 5 hours)
  • Demonstrate safe handgun handling
  • Pass the live-fire qualification
  • Complete and submit a properly filled application
  • Provide classifiable fingerprints

Legal Requirements

You will be denied a license if you:
  • Are prohibited under state or federal law from possessing a firearm
  • Have been convicted of certain crimes
  • Are subject to certain protective orders
  • Have been adjudicated mentally incompetent
  • Are addicted to alcohol or controlled substances

Part II: Avoid Victimization

Remember: The best self-defense is avoiding dangerous situations altogether.

Home Security

  • Install and use quality locks on all doors and windows
  • Use wide-angle viewers on solid doors
  • Install motion-detection exterior lighting
  • Keep shrubbery pruned to eliminate hiding places
  • Never hide keys outside your home
  • Never open your door to strangers
  • Never tell strangers you are home alone

Personal Safety in Public

  • Make eye contact with people you meet - communicates awareness and confidence
  • Be aware of your surroundings at all times
  • Park in well-lit, populated areas
  • Have your keys ready before reaching your vehicle
  • Check your vehicle before entering
  • Trust your instincts - if something feels wrong, leave the area

Mental Readiness

Constantly assess your environment by asking:

  • Are there other people around?
  • Are there things out of place or unusual?
  • Does anyone appear nervous or suspicious?
  • Where would I go for cover if needed?
  • What would I do if confronted right now?
  • Where are the exits?

Vehicle Safety

  • Always lock vehicle doors while driving
  • Be especially alert at intersections and stop lights
  • Leave space between your vehicle and the one ahead
  • If confronted during a carjacking, consider giving up the vehicle rather than your life
  • Never allow yourself to be taken to a secondary location

Principles of Criminal Liability - Culpability

A.C.A. Â§ 5-2-201 - Definitions

  • "Act": A bodily movement
  • "Conduct": An act or omission and its accompanying culpable mental state
  • "Omission": A failure to perform an act as to which a duty of performance is imposed by law

A.C.A. Â§ 5-2-202 - Culpable Mental States

Criminal offenses require both a criminal act AND a culpable mental state (mens rea). Understanding these is critical:

1. PURPOSELY

A person acts purposely with respect to his or her conduct or a result when:

  • It is the person's conscious object to engage in conduct of that nature or to cause that result
  • Example: You aim your gun at someone and pull the trigger intending to shoot them

2. KNOWINGLY

A person acts knowingly with respect to his or her conduct when:

  • The person is aware that his or her conduct is of that nature
  • The person is aware that it is practically certain that conduct will cause a result
  • Example: You know your gun is loaded and you know that pulling the trigger will fire it

3. RECKLESSLY

A person acts recklessly when he or she:

  • Consciously disregards a substantial and unjustifiable risk
  • The risk must be of such nature and degree that disregard constitutes a gross deviation from the standard of care a reasonable person would observe
  • Example: You know the gun might be loaded but you pull the trigger anyway without checking

4. NEGLIGENTLY

A person acts negligently when he or she:

  • Should be aware of a substantial and unjustifiable risk
  • Failure to perceive the risk involves a gross deviation from standard of care a reasonable person would observe
  • Example: You didn't check if the gun was loaded before handling it, and a reasonable person would have checked

Why This Matters for Self-Defense

Your mental state when using force affects both:

  • Whether you're criminally liable: Different crimes require different mental states
  • Whether justification applies: Self-defense requires you reasonably believed force was necessary

Justification as a Defense - A.C.A. Â§Â§ 5-2-601 to -622

Justification is a complete defense to criminal charges. If your use of force is justified under Arkansas law, you cannot be convicted of a crime based on that use of force.

A.C.A. Â§ 5-2-601 - Key Definitions

  • "Deadly Physical Force": Physical force that under the circumstances in which it is used is readily capable of causing death or serious physical injury
    • Using a firearm ALWAYS constitutes deadly physical force
  • "Dwelling": An enclosed space that is used or intended to be used as a human habitation, home, or residence on a temporary or permanent basis
  • "Minor": Any person less than eighteen (18) years of age
  • "Unlawful Physical Force": Physical force that is employed without the consent of the person against whom it is directed and the employment of the physical force constitutes a criminal offense or tort

A.C.A. Â§ 5-2-604 - Choice of Evils

Conduct is justified when:

  • The person reasonably believes the conduct is necessary to avoid imminent harm
  • The desirability and urgency of avoiding the harm clearly outweigh the harm sought to be prevented by the law
  • Example: Breaking into cabin in wilderness to avoid freezing to death

A.C.A. Â§ 5-2-605 - Use of Physical Force Generally

The justification afforded by Â§Â§ 5-2-606 through 5-2-609 is unavailable when:

  • The person uses physical force with purpose to cause death or serious physical injury
  • The person provokes the use of physical force by another person with purpose to cause injury or death
  • The person knows he or she can avoid the necessity of using physical force with complete safety by retreating (except as provided in Â§ 5-2-607)

General Principles of Justification

  • Justification is based on reasonable belief at the time - not what you learn later
  • You can only use the amount of force reasonably necessary
  • If you can safely retreat, you generally should (with important exceptions for deadly force)
  • You cannot claim justification if you started or provoked the conflict

A.C.A. Â§ 5-2-606 - Use of Physical Force in Defense of Person

When Physical Force is Justified

A person is justified in using physical force upon another person to defend:

  • Himself or herself, OR
  • A third person

From what the person reasonably believes to be:

  • The use or imminent use of unlawful physical force by that other person

The person may use a degree of force that he or she reasonably believes to be necessary

CRITICAL LIMITATION

A person may NOT use deadly physical force except as provided in A.C.A. Â§ 5-2-607

Since using a firearm constitutes deadly physical force, you MUST meet the requirements of Â§ 5-2-607

When Physical Force is NOT Justified (Â§ 5-2-606(b))

You are NOT justified in using physical force if:

1. You Provoked the Confrontation

  • With purpose to cause physical injury or death to the other person
  • You provoke the use of unlawful physical force by the other person

Exception: If you in good faith withdraw and effectively communicate your withdrawal, and the other person continues or threatens to continue using force

2. You Are the Initial Aggressor

  • You are the initial aggressor in the encounter

Exception: Same as above - you can regain justification by withdrawing in good faith and communicating that withdrawal

3. Combat by Agreement

  • The physical force involved is the product of combat by agreement not authorized by law
  • Example: Mutual combat, "consensual" fights

Key Concepts

  • "Reasonably believes": Your belief must be objectively reasonable - what would a reasonable person believe?
  • "Imminent": About to happen immediately, not sometime in the future
  • "Unlawful physical force": Force used without consent that constitutes a crime or tort
  • "Necessary": Required in the situation; force must be proportional to threat

A.C.A. Â§ 5-2-607 - Use of Deadly Physical Force

THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT STATUTE FOR ARMED CITIZENS

This statute defines when you may use DEADLY PHYSICAL FORCE (including firearms)

When Deadly Force is Justified (Â§ 5-2-607(a))

A person is justified in using deadly physical force upon another person if the person reasonably believes that the other person is:

1. Committing or About to Commit a Felony Involving Physical Force or Violence

  • Examples: Armed robbery, aggravated assault, kidnapping, rape
  • Must involve force or violence - not all felonies qualify
  • Must be happening now or about to happen (imminent)

2. Using or About to Use Unlawful Deadly Physical Force

  • Attacker has weapon and is threatening to use it
  • Attacker is using force that could cause death/serious injury
  • Must be unlawful - can't shoot police making lawful arrest

3. Imminently Endangering the Person's Life

  • Creating immediate danger of death
  • Example: Attacker choking you, beating your head against concrete

4. About to Victimize from Pattern of Domestic Abuse

  • Continuation of pattern of domestic abuse
  • Requires established pattern, not isolated incident

Stand Your Ground - No Duty to Retreat (Â§ 5-2-607(b))

A person is NOT required to retreat if the person is:

  1. Lawfully present at the location where deadly physical force is used
  2. Has a reasonable belief that the person against whom deadly force is used is imminently threatening to cause death or serious physical injury
  3. Not the initial aggressor and has not provoked the person against whom deadly physical force is used

Additional No Duty to Retreat Situations (Â§ 5-2-607(b))

  • The person is in his or her dwelling or on curtilage surrounding the dwelling and was not the original aggressor
  • The person is a law enforcement officer or assisting law enforcement
Critical Points:
  • Your belief must be REASONABLE - judged objectively
  • Threat must be IMMINENT - happening now or about to happen
  • You cannot be the INITIAL AGGRESSOR or have provoked the situation
  • Even if justified, you may still face arrest, charges, and civil lawsuits

Defense of Premises and Property

A.C.A. Â§ 5-2-608 - Use of Physical Force in Defense of Premises

Non-Deadly Force (Â§ 5-2-608(a))

A person in lawful possession or control of premises or a vehicle is justified in using non-deadly physical force upon another person when and to the extent that the person reasonably believes the use of non-deadly physical force is necessary to:

  • Prevent or terminate the commission or attempted commission of criminal trespass by the other person in or upon the premises or vehicle

Deadly Force (Â§ 5-2-608(b))

A person may use deadly physical force under this section if:

  1. Use of deadly physical force is authorized by A.C.A. Â§ 5-2-607 (self-defense), OR
  2. The person reasonably believes the use of deadly physical force is necessary to prevent the commission of:
    • Arson, or
    • Burglary by a trespasser
Important Distinctions:
  • Criminal Trespass: Unlawfully entering or remaining on property - does NOT alone justify deadly force
  • Burglary: Entering or remaining in building with intent to commit a felony or theft - MAY justify deadly force
  • Arson: Starting a fire to damage property - MAY justify deadly force

A.C.A. Â§ 5-2-609 - Use of Physical Force in Defense of Property

Non-Deadly Force Only

A person is justified in using non-deadly physical force upon another person when and to the extent that the person reasonably believes the use of non-deadly physical force is necessary to prevent or terminate the other person's:

  • Commission or attempted commission of theft or criminal mischief, OR
  • Subsequent flight from the commission or attempted commission of theft or criminal mischief

CRITICAL: You CANNOT Use Deadly Force to Protect Property Alone

  • Cannot shoot someone for stealing your car
  • Cannot shoot someone for vandalizing property
  • Cannot shoot fleeing thief
  • Property is NOT worth a human life
  • Exception: If person committing theft also poses threat justifying deadly force under Â§ 5-2-607

Practical Application

Scenario Deadly Force Justified?
Someone stealing from your yard NO - property theft only
Someone breaking into your garage at night MAYBE - burglary may justify if reasonably believed necessary
Someone breaking into your home at night LIKELY YES - burglary + reasonable fear for safety
Someone in your home threatening you with weapon YES - meets Â§ 5-2-607 requirements

Additional Justification Statutes

A.C.A. Â§ 5-2-611 - Use of Physical Force by Private Person Aiding Law Enforcement

A person who has been directed by a law enforcement officer to assist is justified in using:

  • Reasonable and necessary physical force to effect an arrest, prevent escape, or prevent commission of crime
  • Unless the person knows the law enforcement officer is not authorized to request assistance

A.C.A. Â§ 5-2-612 - Use of Physical Force in Resisting Arrest

A person is NOT JUSTIFIED in using physical force to resist an arrest by law enforcement officer who is:

  • Known or reasonably appears to be authorized to make the arrest, AND
  • The person knows or reasonably should know he or she is being arrested

Even if the arrest is unlawful! Fight it in court, not on the street.

A.C.A. Â§ 5-2-614 - Use of Reckless or Negligent Force

When a person believes that use of physical force is necessary for any purpose justifying that use under this subchapter:

Reckless or Negligent in Forming Belief

If the person is reckless or negligent in forming that belief OR in employing an excessive degree of physical force:

  • The justification is UNAVAILABLE in prosecution for offense for which recklessness or negligence suffices to establish culpable mental state
  • Example: You think you see a gun but were reckless in that belief - you may be charged with negligent homicide even though you thought force was justified

Injury to Third Party

When a person is justified in using physical force but recklessly or negligently injures or creates substantial risk of injury to a third party:

  • The justification is UNAVAILABLE in prosecution for recklessness or negligence toward the third party
  • Example: You're justified in shooting attacker, but you miss and hit innocent bystander - you may be charged for injury to bystander

A.C.A. Â§ 5-2-620 - Use of Force to Defend Persons and Property Within Home

This is Arkansas's "Castle Doctrine" law providing enhanced protection for use of force in the home.

A.C.A. Â§ 5-2-621 - Attempting to Protect Persons During Commission of Felony

No person is civilly liable for action or omission intended to protect himself or another from personal injury during commission of a felony, UNLESS:

  • The action or omission itself constitutes a felony

This provides some civil immunity for reasonable defensive actions, but does not protect felonious conduct

Assault and Battery - A.C.A. Â§Â§ 5-13-201 to -310

Understanding Assault vs. Battery:

  • Battery: Unlawfully causing physical injury or seriously physically annoying contact
  • Assault: Purposely creating apprehension of imminent physical injury or engaging in conduct creating substantial risk

A.C.A. Â§ 5-13-201 - Battery in the First Degree

Class B Felony (5-20 years imprisonment)

A person commits battery in the first degree if:

  1. With purpose of causing serious physical injury to another person, the person causes serious physical injury to any person by means of a deadly weapon; OR
  2. The person purposely causes serious physical injury to:
    • A person twelve (12) years of age or younger
    • A person the defendant knows or reasonably should know is pregnant

A.C.A. Â§ 5-13-202 - Battery in the Second Degree

Class D Felony (0-6 years imprisonment)

A person commits battery in the second degree if the person:

  1. Recklessly causes serious physical injury to another person by means of a deadly weapon;
  2. Purposely causes physical injury to another person by means of a deadly weapon;
  3. Purposely causes physical injury to:
    • Law enforcement officer, firefighter, code enforcement officer, etc.
    • Teacher or other school employee
    • Employee of a detention facility, jail, or correctional facility
    • Healthcare worker
    • Park ranger or wildlife officer
    • Pregnant woman (known or should know)
    • Child twelve (12) years of age or younger

A.C.A. Â§ 5-13-203 - Battery in the Third Degree

Class A Misdemeanor (up to 1 year jail, up to $2,500 fine)

A person commits battery in the third degree if the person:

  • Recklessly causes physical injury to another person;
  • Negligently causes physical injury to another person by means of a deadly weapon; OR
  • Purposely causes stupor, unconsciousness, or physical or mental impairment or injury by administering a drug or substance without consent

Assault Crimes

A.C.A. Â§ 5-13-204 - Aggravated Assault

Class D Felony (0-6 years imprisonment)

A person commits aggravated assault if, under circumstances manifesting extreme indifference to the value of human life, the person purposely:

  1. Engages in conduct that creates a substantial danger of death or serious physical injury to another person; OR
  2. Displays a firearm in such a manner that creates a substantial danger of death or serious physical injury
Important: Merely displaying a firearm can constitute aggravated assault if done recklessly or without justification!

A person commits assault in the second degree if the person recklessly engages in conduct that creates a substantial risk of physical injury to another person by means of a deadly weapon.

A.C.A. Â§ 5-13-207 - Assault in the Third Degree

A person commits assault in the third degree if the person:

  1. Recklessly engages in conduct that creates substantial risk of physical injury to another person;
  2. Purposely creates apprehension of imminent physical injury to another person; OR
  3. In the course of consummating or attempting to consummate a theft or unauthorized taking of property, the person uses or threatens to use force against another person with purpose of preventing or overcoming resistance

Criminal and Civil Liability Summary

Potential Criminal Charges

If you make an error in judgment about using deadly force, you may be charged with:

  • Homicide offenses (A.C.A. Â§Â§ 5-10-101 to -106)
    • Murder in the First Degree (Capital Murder or Class Y Felony)
    • Murder in the Second Degree (Class A Felony)
    • Manslaughter (Class C Felony)
    • Negligent Homicide (Class A or B Misdemeanor)
  • Assault and Battery (A.C.A. Â§Â§ 5-13-201 to -310) - as detailed in previous slides
  • Weapons offenses (A.C.A. Â§Â§ 5-73-101 to -133) - to be covered next

Civil Liability - A.C.A. Â§ 16-118-107

Civil Action by Crime Victim:

Even if you are NOT convicted criminally (or charges are never filed), you may still face civil liability:

  • Victim or their estate can file civil lawsuit for damages
  • Lower burden of proof: Preponderance of evidence (more likely than not) vs. beyond reasonable doubt
  • Plaintiff may recover actual damages (medical bills, lost wages, pain and suffering)
  • Plaintiff may recover costs and attorney's fees
  • You could lose your home, savings, retirement, and other assets
  • Homeowner's insurance typically does NOT cover intentional acts

Limited Civil Immunity - A.C.A. Â§ 16-120-303

Attempting to Protect Persons During Commission of Felony:

No person is civilly liable for action or omission intended to protect himself or herself or another from personal injury during commission of a felony, UNLESS:

  • The action or omission itself constitutes a felony

This provides some protection, but does not eliminate all civil liability risk.

A.C.A. Â§ 5-73-322(j) - Campus Carry Immunity

Public universities, colleges, and community colleges are immune from claims for monetary damages arising from licensee's use of, or failure to use, a concealed handgun.

BUT: The licensee is NOT immune and may be held personally liable.

Bottom Line

Using a firearm, even in what you believe is legitimate self-defense, can result in:

  • Criminal arrest and prosecution
  • Years or life in prison if convicted
  • Civil lawsuits for damages
  • Financial ruin - legal fees, judgments, loss of assets
  • Loss of gun rights
  • Permanent criminal record

Use deadly force ONLY as an absolute last resort when you reasonably believe your life or the life of another is in imminent danger.

A.C.A. Â§ 5-73-120 - Carrying a Weapon

Class A Misdemeanor - Up to 1 year jail and/or up to $2,500 fine

The Offense

A person commits the offense of carrying a weapon if the person possesses a handgun, knife, or club:

  • On or about his or her person
  • In a vehicle occupied by the person
  • Otherwise readily available for use
  • WITH A PURPOSE to employ the handgun, knife, or club as a weapon against a person

Key Points

  • Presumption of Lawful Purpose: A person is PRESUMED to be carrying a weapon with a lawful purpose
  • Prosecution must prove purpose to employ as weapon against a person
  • Simply having a weapon is NOT the crime - it's the intent that matters

  2. Person is law enforcement officer, member of armed forces, member of municipal fire department bomb squad (Â§ 12-15-204)
  3. Person is in personal vehicle or on property in which person has possessory or proprietary interest
  4. Person is on a journey (has left home for any purpose or duration)
  5. Person is a security guard in course of duties
  6. Person is hunting or fishing in area where legal
  7. Person is certified law enforcement officer (on or off duty)
  8. Person is licensed to carry concealed handgun under Â§Â§ 5-73-301 et seq.
  9. Person is participating in firearms-related activity or training
  10. Person is traveling to or from such activity with unloaded weapon

Part V: Inspection of the Handgun

Inspect your handgun EVERY TIME before carrying it

Pre-Carry Inspection Checklist

  1. Barrel
    • Unload and open the handgun
    • Look inside barrel for dirt or obstructions
    • Even small obstructions can cause catastrophic failure
  2. Interior Surfaces
    • Check for dirt or rust
    • Clean if necessary
  3. Moving Parts
    • Revolvers: Check ejector for ease of movement and binding
    • Semi-automatics: Check slide for smooth operation
  4. Hammer
    • Move hammer to cocked position
    • Check hammer well for dirt, rust, or obstructions
    • Clean with toothpick if needed
  5. Trigger Operation
    • Test trigger pull (with unloaded gun)
    • Should be smooth without catching or grinding
  6. Overall Condition
    • Check for cracks or damage
    • Ensure all parts are secure
    • Verify sights are intact
NEVER carry a handgun that is:
  • Dirty or corroded
  • Damaged or has loose parts
  • Has not been recently inspected
  • Malfunctioning in any way

Part VI: Types of Handguns - Revolvers

Definition

A handgun is any firearm, other than a fully automatic firearm, with a barrel length less than 12 inches that is designed to be fired with one hand.

Revolver Basics

A revolver holds ammunition in a rotating cylinder and can be fired with the hammer forward or cocked.

Revolver Parts

  • Frame: Main body including hammer, hammer spur, and cylinder release
  • Cylinder: Rotating part that holds ammunition (typically 5-6 rounds)
  • Barrel: Through which the bullet travels
  • Grip: Handle portion

Single Action Operation

  • Hammer must be manually cocked before each shot
  • Lighter trigger pull - more accurate but more dangerous
  • Slight bump can cause discharge when cocked
  • Best for target shooting, not self-defense

Double Action Operation

  • Trigger pull cocks and releases hammer
  • Heavier trigger pull - safer for stress situations
  • Requires intentional action to fire
  • Recommended for concealed carry

Loading a Revolver (Right-handed)

  1. Point firearm toward ground
  2. Release cylinder latch and swing cylinder out
  3. Place rounds in chambers with right hand
  4. Close cylinder gently - listen for snap/click
  5. Rotate cylinder to ensure it locks
  6. NEVER slam cylinder shut - damages the gun!

Unloading a Revolver

  1. Keep barrel pointed safe direction
  2. Open cylinder
  3. Point barrel upward
  4. Press ejector rod - rounds should fall out
  5. Visually verify all chambers are empty
  6. Leave cylinder open when setting down

Mechanical Safeties

  • Transfer Bar: Prevents firing unless trigger pulled
  • Hammer Blocks: Blocks hammer from striking firing pin

Types of Handguns - Semi-Automatic Pistols

Semi-Automatic Basics

When fired, a semi-automatic pistol automatically extracts and ejects the empty case and chambers a new round with each trigger pull.

Semi-Automatic Parts

  • Frame: Main body including trigger, hammer, and safety
  • Slide: Top portion that moves during firing cycle
  • Barrel: Through which bullet travels
  • Grip: Handle portion (houses magazine)
  • Magazine: Removable container that holds ammunition

Loading a Semi-Automatic

  1. Hold handgun in firing grip, pointed safe direction
  2. Insert loaded magazine until it clicks
  3. Grasp slide and pull fully to rear
  4. Release slide - it will chamber a round
  5. Apply safety if equipped

Unloading a Semi-Automatic

  1. Point in safe direction
  2. Press magazine release and remove magazine
  3. Pull slide to rear and lock open
  4. Visually and physically check chamber is empty
  5. Release slide or holster with slide locked back

Types of Safeties

  • Manual Safety: Lever that blocks trigger or firing pin
  • Grip Safety: Must be depressed to fire
  • Trigger Safety: Part of trigger that must be depressed
  • Firing Pin Block: Prevents firing pin from moving forward
Remember: Safeties are mechanical devices and CAN FAIL. Always follow all safety rules regardless of safety status.

Single Action vs. Double Action

  • Single Action: Trigger only releases hammer (hammer must be cocked first)
  • Double Action: Trigger cocks and releases hammer
  • DA/SA: First shot double action, subsequent shots single action
  • Striker-Fired: No external hammer, consistent trigger pull

Part VII: Ammunition

Four Basic Components

  1. Case: Holds all components together (usually brass)
  2. Bullet: The projectile (usually lead, may have jacket)
  3. Powder: Explosive propellant
  4. Primer: Small charge that ignites the powder

Common Bullet Types

Type Characteristics Recommendation
Wad Cutter Flat end, low penetration, makes clean holes in paper targets Practice only - NOT for carry
Ball/FMJ Round nose, high penetration, passes through target NOT recommended - dangerous to bystanders
Hollow Point Expands on impact, good stopping power, less over-penetration RECOMMENDED for carry
Armor Piercing Hardened steel, extreme penetration, poor stopping power NOT recommended - dangerous over-penetration

Matching Ammunition to Your Handgun

CRITICAL SAFETY:
  • Always use ammunition that matches your firearm's caliber marking
  • Using wrong ammunition can cause catastrophic failure, injury, or death
  • Check ammunition before loading EVERY TIME
  • When in doubt, consult the owner's manual or a gunsmith

Reloads/Remanufactured Ammunition

  • Can be reliable if loaded properly
  • Quality varies greatly
  • Some manufacturers void warranty if reloads are used
  • For carry, use factory ammunition from reputable manufacturers

How Ammunition Works

  1. Trigger releases hammer
  2. Hammer strikes firing pin
  3. Firing pin strikes primer
  4. Primer ignites powder
  5. Expanding gases propel bullet through barrel

Ammunition Storage

  • Store in cool, dry place
  • Keep away from heat sources and moisture
  • Store separately from firearms
  • Keep out of reach of children
  • Inspect periodically for corrosion or damage

Part IX: Carrying Concealed

What Does "Concealed" Mean?

"Concealed" means to cover from observation to prevent public view.

Your handgun must be completely hidden from ordinary observation. "Printing" (outline showing through clothing) should be minimized.

Choosing Your Carry Method

Consider these factors:

  • Weapon size and type - larger guns harder to conceal
  • Body type - what works for others may not work for you
  • Clothing style - dress code limitations
  • Climate - seasonal clothing changes
  • Accessibility - can you draw quickly if needed?
  • Comfort - will you actually carry it?
  • Retention - gun stays secure during normal activity

Common Carry Methods

Method Advantages Disadvantages
Inside Waistband (IWB) Good concealment, comfortable with proper holster Requires larger pants, can be uncomfortable sitting
Outside Waistband (OWB) Comfortable, easy draw Harder to conceal, requires cover garment
Appendix Carry Fast access, good concealment Uncomfortable for some, safety concerns
Shoulder Holster Comfortable for long periods, works with suits Requires jacket, muzzle crosses others during draw
Ankle Holster Deep concealment, backup gun option Slow draw, limited to small guns, difficult while seated
Pocket Carry Very concealable, casual Limited to very small guns, requires pocket holster
Belly Band Versatile, works with athletic wear Less secure, can be uncomfortable
Purse/Bag Comfortable, no special clothing needed Slow access, can be separated from you, NOT recommended
Off-Body Carry Warning: Carrying in purse, bag, or backpack is generally NOT recommended because:
  • Can be set down or taken from you
  • Slower access in emergency
  • Children or others may access
  • If you must use off-body carry, use dedicated concealed carry purse with locking compartment

Practice!

  • Practice drawing from concealment (with UNLOADED gun)
  • Practice re-holstering safely
  • Practice at home in private
  • Ensure you can access firearm while seated, standing, or moving
  • Time to draw is NOT the time to figure out your holster

Part XII: Handgun Safety Rules

The Four Cardinal Rules of Firearm Safety

  1. Treat ALL firearms as if they are ALWAYS loaded
  2. NEVER point a firearm at anything you are not willing to destroy
  3. Keep your finger OFF the trigger until ready to shoot
  4. Be sure of your target and what is beyond it

Essential Safety Rules for Concealed Carry

  1. A gun is ALWAYS loaded - NEVER ASSUME!
    • Always personally verify the gun is unloaded
  2. Secure your handgun from children
    • Even well-behaved children are curious
    • "Hidden" is never hidden well enough
  3. Set all safety locks after loading
    • Remember: Safeties are mechanical and can fail
  4. Store firearms and ammunition separately
    • In locations inaccessible to unauthorized persons
  5. NEVER point a handgun at anyone you are not prepared to kill
    • There are no "warning shots"
    • Once you point a gun, you've made a lethal force decision
  6. NEVER remove your handgun from holster unless needed to protect life
    • Your life or the life of another person
  7. NEVER put finger in trigger guard until ready to shoot
    • Keep finger indexed along frame/slide
  8. NEVER carry in the cocked position
    • Applies primarily to single-action firearms
  9. NEVER show off or handle handgun in public
    • Must remain concealed unless life in danger
  10. NEVER use handgun as a threat or display it to intimidate
    • Brandishing is a crime
    • Use only as absolute last resort
  11. NEVER practice anywhere except approved locations
    • Authorized ranges only
  12. NEVER carry when drinking alcohol or using impairing drugs
    • Includes prescription and over-the-counter medications that affect judgment or coordination

Part XIII: Firearms Range Safety Rules

Strict discipline must be maintained at ALL times on the range. Violations may result in immediate removal from the course.

General Range Rules

  1. ALWAYS follow Range Master instructions
  2. Upon arrival, place handgun on bench UNLOADED
    • Cylinder open for revolvers
    • Slide locked back for semi-automatics
    • Step back for Range Master to inspect
  3. Weapons remain in holsters except on firing line
  4. No "dry firing" except under Range Officer supervision
  5. NEVER load firearm unless on firing line and commanded to do so
  6. Do not speak to anyone on firing line
    • Except Range Officer, coaching, or being coached
  7. If spoken to on firing line, do NOT turn around to reply
  8. All weapons pointed down range when loading
  9. Check barrel for obstructions before loading
  10. Misfire or malfunction - keep gun pointed downrange, call Range Officer

Commands and Procedures

  • "Step to the line" - Move to firing line, DO NOT TOUCH GUN OR AMMUNITION
  • "The line is HOT" - Range Master declares, you may now handle firearms
  • "Load" - Load your firearm
  • "Commence fire" - Begin shooting exercise
  • "Cease fire" - IMMEDIATELY stop shooting, keep gun pointed downrange
  • "Unload and show clear" - Unload and show Range Officer gun is empty
  • "The line is COLD" - All guns are unloaded and verified, may move downrange

Additional Safety Rules

  • NEVER lay down a loaded firearm
  • NEVER move toward targets without Range Master permission
  • Be careful not to drop ammunition
  • Horseplay is STRICTLY FORBIDDEN
  • If you have problems, raise free hand and call for assistance
  • Eye and ear protection REQUIRED at all times
EMERGENCY CEASE FIRE: Anyone may call "CEASE FIRE" if unsafe condition observed. ALL shooting stops immediately. All guns remain pointed downrange.

Part XIV: Shooting the Weapon - Fundamentals

B.R.A.S.S. - Key to Accurate Shooting

  1. B - Breathe
    • Take normal breath, exhale partially
    • Hold remaining breath while aiming and shooting
    • Don't hold breath more than 10 seconds
    • If more time needed, release and repeat
  2. R - Relax
    • Tension causes poor shots
    • Firm grip but relaxed body
    • Don't anticipate recoil
  3. A - Aim
    • Proper sight alignment and sight picture
    • Focus on front sight
    • Target should be slightly fuzzy
  4. S - Slack
    • Take up trigger slack (free play)
    • Prepare for smooth press
  5. S - Squeeze
    • Smooth, steady trigger press
    • Should surprise you when gun fires
    • Prevents jerking and flinching

Stance

  • Isosceles: Face target squarely, both arms extended equally
  • Weaver: Bladed stance, push-pull with arms
  • Modified Weaver: Combination of both
  • Feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent
  • Lean slightly forward
  • Find what's comfortable and natural for YOU

Grip

Proper grip is CRITICAL for accuracy and control
  • Grip as high on the backstrap as possible
  • Strong hand thumb along frame
  • Support hand fills gap, thumbs forward
  • Firm pressure - 60% support hand, 40% strong hand
  • Grip should stop trembling but not cause fatigue
  • Consistent grip every time

Shooting Fundamentals (Continued)

Sight Picture

For proper sight picture:

  • Front sight top level with rear sight top
  • Equal white space (light bars) on each side of front sight
  • Front sight should appear to "sit" on target at 6 o'clock position
  • Focus on FRONT SIGHT - target and rear sight will be slightly blurry

Sight Alignment

  • Most critical element of accurate shooting
  • Small error at sights = large miss at target
  • 1/10 inch error at sights = 15 inch miss at 25 yards
  • Perfect alignment more important than perfect sight picture

Trigger Control

Proper trigger control prevents disturbing sight alignment:

  • Use pad of index finger (not joint, not tip)
  • Press straight to the rear
  • Smooth, steady increase in pressure
  • Should not know exact moment gun will fire
  • This prevents flinching and jerking
  • Follow through - maintain pressure after shot
  • Reset trigger for next shot

Common Shooting Errors

Error Cause Correction
Flinching/Anticipating Bracing for recoil before shot Focus on smooth trigger press, dry fire practice
Jerking Trigger Too fast/hard trigger pull Slow, smooth press - surprise break
Heeling Pushing with heel of hand Relax grip, focus on trigger finger only
Thumbing Tightening thumb during trigger pull Relax thumb, keep along frame

Breathing Control

  • Breathing causes vertical movement of sights
  • Take normal breath
  • Exhale to comfortable position
  • Hold breath while taking shot
  • Don't hold longer than 10 seconds
  • If needed, breathe and start over

"Dry Firing" Practice

  • Practice trigger control without ammunition
  • ALWAYS verify gun is completely empty first
  • Remove ALL ammunition from area
  • Point at safe backstop (not walls or windows)
  • Excellent for developing trigger control and sight picture
  • Free practice you can do at home

Malfunctions and Safety Considerations

Misfires and Hangfires

CRITICAL SAFETY: If gun doesn't fire when trigger is pulled:
  1. Keep gun pointed at target for 10 seconds
  2. Then carefully lower gun and call for assistance
  3. NEVER immediately open action - could be hangfire
  • Misfire: Primer fails to ignite powder - gun doesn't fire
  • Hangfire: Delayed ignition - may fire within 5 seconds
  • Treat EVERY failure to fire as potential hangfire

Common Semi-Automatic Malfunctions

Malfunction Description Clearance
Failure to Feed Round doesn't chamber Tap-Rack-Assess
Failure to Eject Empty case stays in chamber Rack slide to eject
Stovepipe Case caught in ejection port Sweep away and rack slide
Double Feed Two rounds try to enter chamber Lock slide back, remove magazine, clear chamber

Ricochet Dangers

Bullets can bounce off hard surfaces and travel in unpredictable directions:

  • Pavement, concrete, or asphalt
  • Rocks or stones
  • Metal surfaces
  • Water (bullets can skip across water)
  • Building materials
Always consider: Where will the bullet go if it ricochets? Who or what is in that direction?

Over-Penetration/Pass-Through

Bullets can pass through:

  • Human bodies (especially with FMJ ammunition)
  • Interior walls (drywall offers minimal resistance)
  • Doors
  • Furniture
  • Multiple floors in buildings
You are responsible for EVERY bullet that leaves your gun - even if it passes through intended target and strikes someone else.

Warning Shots

WARNING SHOTS ARE:
  • Dangerous - bullet must land somewhere
  • Illegal in most circumstances
  • Tactically unsound - wastes ammunition
  • Can escalate situation
  • NEVER FIRE WARNING SHOTS

Legal Use of Force - Scenarios

Remember: Every situation is unique. These scenarios are for discussion only. You must make your own decisions based on the specific circumstances you face.

Scenario 1: Home Invasion

Situation: 2:00 AM, you hear glass breaking downstairs. You're upstairs with your family.

Considerations:

  • Are you in immediate danger?
  • Can you secure your family in safe room and call 911?
  • Do you need to go downstairs or defend your position?
  • Do you have duty to retreat in your own home? (No - Castle Doctrine)
  • Can you reasonably believe intruder poses threat of death or serious injury?

Scenario 2: Parking Lot Carjacking

Situation: Someone approaches with knife demanding your car keys.

Considerations:

  • Is knife displayed deadly force? (Yes)
  • Are you in immediate danger? (Likely yes)
  • Is car worth your life? (No)
  • Can you escape safely by giving keys? (Possibly)
  • Are there children in the car? (Changes analysis)
  • Are you being forced into vehicle? (Never go to secondary location)

Scenario 3: Confrontation at Gas Station

Situation: Argument escalates, person becomes aggressive and threatening but unarmed.

Considerations:

  • Is verbal threat sufficient for deadly force? (No)
  • Are you the initial aggressor? (Loses justification)
  • Can you leave the area? (Always best option)
  • Are you in reasonable fear of death/serious injury? (Unlikely if unarmed)
  • Would displaying gun escalate situation? (Probably)

Scenario 4: Witnessing Armed Robbery

Situation: You see someone robbing convenience store at gunpoint.

Considerations:

  • Are YOU in immediate danger? (Maybe not)
  • Can you defend others under A.C.A. Â§ 5-2-607? (Yes, if reasonable belief of threat)
  • Is property worth taking life? (No)
  • Could your intervention make situation worse?
  • Are you sure who the aggressor is?
  • Could you be mistaken for the criminal by police?
  • Best action: Be good witness, call 911
Key Principles:
  • Avoidance is always best option
  • Your gun is to protect life, not property
  • Consider consequences before acting
  • Be absolutely certain deadly force is justified
  • When in doubt, do NOT shoot

Responsibilities of License Holders

Legal Responsibilities

  • Know and obey ALL applicable laws (state, federal, local)
  • Stay current on law changes
  • Carry license whenever carrying handgun
  • Present license when requested by law enforcement (if carrying)
  • Notify law enforcement you're armed (if carrying)
  • Report any disqualifying events to ASP within 10 days
  • Renew license before expiration
  • Notify ASP of address changes within 30 days

Moral and Ethical Responsibilities

  • Treat firearm ownership as serious responsibility
  • Continue training and education
  • Practice regularly to maintain proficiency
  • Secure firearms from unauthorized access
  • Set good example for other gun owners
  • Avoid confrontations and dangerous situations
  • Never drink alcohol while carrying
  • Be sober ambassador for 2nd Amendment rights

Continuing Education

Your training doesn't end with this course:

  • Take advanced training courses
  • Practice regularly at the range
  • Study use of force laws and court cases
  • Consider force-on-force scenario training
  • Join shooting organizations (USCCA, NRA, etc.)
  • Stay informed about legal changes

Final Thoughts

Carrying a concealed handgun is a serious responsibility that should not be taken lightly. With your license comes great power and great responsibility.

  • Be aware of your surroundings
  • Avoid dangerous situations
  • De-escalate confrontations
  • Use deadly force only as absolute last resort
  • If you must shoot, shoot to stop the threat
  • Call 911 immediately after any defensive use
